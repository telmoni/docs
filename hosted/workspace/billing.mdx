Billing applies to the hosted service, and to each organization rather than
each person: the organization is the unit of billing, and its projects,
members and API keys are covered by its plan. An organization's Owner and
Admins see its plan beside the organization's name in the header's switcher,
and on its **Billing** page (`/{organization}/billing`), which links to the
public **Plans** page (`/plans`). **Billing** is in the organization's
sidebar, before **Settings**, and `g b` reaches it.

## The Hobby plan

Every organization starts on the **Hobby** plan, which is free: no card, no
expiry, and no payment details asked for until it moves to a paid plan.

## The plans

The Plans page posts the three plans: Hobby, free; Pro and Teams, each one
flat monthly price; packs of more capacity on either paid plan; and one
add-on, SSO, on Teams alone. It posts what each plan holds, what a pack adds,
and what happens past each limit. Every feature is on every plan, members are
never sold, and nothing is metered: a bill is the plan and the packs an
organization holds, and it does not move on its own.

Any limit Hobby gains, and any pack that goes on sale, is listed here as well,
with what Hobby includes and what more of it costs, before it applies to any
organization.

## Changing plans

Moving up, to Pro from Hobby or to Teams from Pro, applies at once: a first
plan is bought on Stripe's Checkout page, and a move from Pro to Teams
changes the subscription in place, prorated for the rest of the period, with
no card asked for again.

Moving down, from Teams to Pro, takes effect when the current period ends,
as a subscription you are used to does: the organization keeps Teams, and
everything it holds, until then. Its **Billing** page names the day and
offers **Keep Teams**, which takes the move back any time before it. A
cancellation drops a pending move, since it ends the period first.

Cancelling runs to the end of the period, then the organization falls to
Hobby. An organization that could not fall there — another of yours holds
your Hobby place, as below — is refused the cancellation, and its **Billing**
page names the ways out instead: delete it, merge its projects into your
Hobby organization, or hand it to someone whose Hobby place is free.

## One Hobby organization

While Pro and Teams are for sale, a person has one free organization, their
**Hobby place**, as Vercel holds an account to one Hobby team: it is held by
their Hobby organization, or kept by a paid one set to fall to Hobby when its
period ends. Every other organization of theirs is paid for before it exists.
Being a member of other people's organizations is never limited.

- **Your first organization** is your Hobby organization.
- **Another you create** while something of yours holds your Hobby place is
  paid for first: the dialog that creates it asks whether it starts on Pro or
  Teams, sends you to Stripe's payment page, and the organization is created
  the moment the payment is confirmed, and opens. Nothing is created, and
  nothing charged, if you leave the payment page.
- **A paid organization you cancel** keeps its plan to the end of its period,
  then falls to Hobby, and holds your Hobby place from the moment you set the
  cancellation; taking the cancellation back frees the place. While another of
  yours holds the place, the cancellation is refused, since the organization
  could not fall to Hobby: delete it, merge it into your Hobby organization,
  or hand it over instead, as its **Billing** page says.
- **A card that fails** until Stripe's retries run out ends the plan all the
  same. The organization falls to Hobby when your Hobby place is free; when
  another of yours holds it, the organization is **paused**: it keeps its
  projects, its data and its members, and stays readable, exportable and
  deletable, but takes in nothing new — exports to the OpenTelemetry
  endpoint, questions to the console agent and invitations are refused with a
  `402` that names Pro — until it is on Pro or Teams again, merged into your
  Hobby organization, handed to someone whose place is free, or, once your
  place frees, moved to Hobby from its **Billing** page. Nothing resumes on its
  own.
- **Handing an organization over:** one on Hobby, paused, or set to fall to
  Hobby goes only to an owner with no organization on Hobby or set to fall to
  it; the offer, or its acceptance, is otherwise refused, naming Pro. A paid
  organization goes to anyone. A paused one handed to someone whose place is
  free takes that place and resumes.
- **Merging:** the owner of a paid or paused organization can merge it into
  their Hobby organization from its **Billing** page. Every project moves
  across, with its API keys, its data and the people on it, and the emptied
  organization is deleted, confirmed with a code emailed to its owner.

Each change is written to the organization's audit log and raised in its
notifications.

## One member on Hobby

While Pro and Teams are for sale, an organization on Hobby holds one member,
its owner, as Vercel's Hobby team holds one person. An invitation from it,
to the organization or to one of its projects, is refused with a `402` that
names Pro, and one sent while the organization was on a paid plan seats
nobody past that once it has fallen to Hobby: the invitation stays live for
when the organization moves back. Nobody is ever removed: an organization
that falls to Hobby keeps every member it has. Its **Members** pages say so
in place of the invite button. A paid organization holds as many members as
it invites, and being a member of other people's organizations is never
limited.

## Self-hosted installations

A self-hosted installation has no plans, no usage limits and no billing.
