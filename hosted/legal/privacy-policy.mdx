This policy describes what the service collects, why, and what happens to it.
The short version: we store what is needed to run your organization and bill
you, we do not sell any of it or train models on it, and deleting your account
or an organization deletes its data.

the service. If this policy changes materially we will update this page and

The service is not directed to children under 16, and we do not knowingly

## What we collect

Account data: your email address and name, received from the identity provider
you sign in with. We never see or store a password.

Billing data: nothing is for sale today, so no payment details are collected.
A plan, once bought, is processed by Stripe: we store the subscription's
status and period and Stripe's identifiers for it, while Stripe holds your
payment card and your invoice history, and we never see the card number.

Platform data: your organizations — the one provisioned for you, named after
the first word of your name until it is renamed, and any you create — with
their names and URLs, and the projects you create, and what you name them, who
belongs to each and at what role, the addresses you send invitations to, and
the names of the API keys you mint.

Telemetry data: what the SDKs in your own code, or an exporter you point at
the ingest endpoint, send about your agents' runs, kept as metadata: each
run's id, the agent's name, the models and tools it called, token counts,
timings, statuses, an error's type, the names of its steps, the tags you set,
the id you give a session, and the id you give the end user a run served,
which is replaced by a keyed hash before it is stored. Prompts, completions,
system instructions, tool inputs and outputs, retrieved documents, error
messages and any attribute that names a person are dropped before anything is
stored, on every project, whose content mode is `off`, the only mode offered
today; the [ingest page](/docs/api/ingest) lists what is kept and what is
dropped, and your project's **Privacy** page shows the same list. The service
executes nothing on your behalf.

Console agent data: questions you ask the console agent and its answers. A
person's conversations with the agent are visible only to them. To answer a
question, the question, the earlier turns of its conversation and what the
agent's tools read for it are sent to the model provider the deployment
configures; the hosted service's is named on the
[Subprocessors](/docs/legal/subprocessors) page.

Operational data: service logs, usage metrics, and an append-only audit trail
of actions taken in your organization, attributed to the acting member.

Automatically collected data: when you use the website or API we receive
technical information such as your IP address, device and browser type, and
request metadata, recorded in logs used to operate and secure the service.

## Cookies and tracking

We use a small number of strictly necessary cookies, chiefly to keep you
signed in and to remember which organization you are working in. Without them
the product cannot function. One more cookie, kept for a year, remembers
whether you collapsed the console's sidebar, and your browser's local storage
keeps your theme, corners, keyboard-shortcut, motion, contrast and focus
preferences and the pages you visited recently. We do not use advertising
cookies or third-party cross-site trackers.

You can set your browser to refuse cookies, but the authenticated product will
not work without the session cookie.

## How we use it

To operate the service: enforcing your project's access rules, applying your
plan's entitlements, showing you your agents' runs as traces, sessions
and users, delivering the notifications you subscribe to, securing
the platform, sending transactional email (invitations, organization and
project transfer offers and their outcomes, and email-change and deletion
confirmation codes), and analyzing how the product is used so we can improve
it.

We do not sell your data, share it for cross-context behavioral advertising,
use your content for advertising, or train machine-learning models on it.

## Retention and deletion

An organization's data is retained while the organization is active. Deleting
an organization closes it at once: nobody in it can use it, its API keys stop
working, and its subscription is cancelled and refunded. A verified cascade
then hard-deletes its projects, memberships, pending invitations, API keys,
notifications, audit log exports, agent conversations and search index, and
its projects' telemetry — every span and every hour of totals, with their
settings and hashing keys — across every service; it deletes nobody's
account, and it cannot be undone.

Telemetry is kept only while its project is active: for 360 days after it
arrives, dropped a day at a time once every span in that day is older, and
the hourly totals derived from it, which name no user and no tag, for 13
months. Deleting a project, or an organization with its projects, marks every
span and every hour of totals of the project deleted the moment the purge
runs, so that within moments nothing reads them again, and the nightly
maintenance removes them from disk; the project's telemetry settings and the key its end-user ids were
hashed under go with the rest of its data. Deleted telemetry then leaves our
backups as they expire, within a further 30 days.

Deleting your account erases your sessions, your memberships, the audit log
exports you started and your record at the identity provider, together with
every organization you own alone.
While you own an organization other people belong to, you hand it to one of
them or remove them first. It cannot be undone either. Deleting an account
deletes that person's conversations and search index rows, and rewrites their
name and address where other people's agent answers quoted them.

A person's conversations with the agent are visible only to them, and kept 90
days after the conversation was last used. The agent's search index holds copies
of the audit log, feed notices, deliveries and each person's answered
questions. It keeps audit copies 730 days, feed copies 90 days and delivery
copies 30 days; the audit log itself is kept without a time limit, including
the addresses invitations were sent to.

An export of the audit log can be seen and downloaded only by the person who
started it, and only while they are an owner or admin of its organization. Its
file is kept 7 days after it is built, then deleted, and leaves our backups as
they expire, within a further 30 days. A file already downloaded is the
organization's, and we cannot recall it.

Erasure falls due the moment you confirm deleting an organization or your
account. We complete it within 30 days of its falling due, and usually within
the hour; deleted data then leaves our backups as they expire, within a further
30 days.

Notices in an organization's feed that named you, such as a note that you
joined a project, are rewritten to name a former member when your account is
deleted. Copies already delivered to a Slack channel or a webhook the
organization connected are the organization's, and we cannot recall them.

The compliance audit log is append-only and retained after deletion. Deleting
an organization removes our billing record for it; the invoice history is
held by Stripe. Operational logs are retained for a limited period and then
rotated out.

## Your privacy rights

Without contacting anyone, you can see your account and organization
information in the console, change your email address if you sign in with a
password, change an organization's name and URL if you own or administer it,
read your organization's identity and a project's roster through the API,
revoke API keys at any time, and delete your account or an organization you
own, and with them their data. Your name comes from your identity provider and
is changed there.

Depending on where you live, you may also have rights under the GDPR or US
state privacy laws (including in California, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas,
Virginia, and others) to confirm, access, correct, delete, or port your
personal information, and to opt out of sale or targeted advertising, neither
of which we do. To exercise a right we cannot self-serve in the product,
contact us at hello@telmoni.com; we may need to verify your identity, and we
will not discriminate against you for exercising a right. If you are in the EU
or UK, you also have the right to lodge a complaint with your local
data-protection authority.

## Security

Tenant data sits behind database-enforced row-level security in FORCE mode,
API keys are stored only as hashes, the types carrying our own service
credentials mask themselves in logs by construction, and traffic is encrypted
in transit. The only credentials of yours the service holds are your
connectors' Slack grants and webhook addresses and signing secrets, sealed at
rest. The end-user ids your telemetry carries are stored
only as keyed hashes, each project's key sealed in a managed key service, and
prompts and completions are dropped before anything is stored. The
[Security page](/docs/legal/security) describes the posture in detail.
