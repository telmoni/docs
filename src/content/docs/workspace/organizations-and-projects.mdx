Telmoni uses a two-tier tenancy model: **Organizations** and **Projects**.
There are no teams.

An **Organization** is the top-level tenant. It holds projects, organization-level
members, organization settings, and a rolled-up audit log.

A **Project** lives inside an organization. It holds API tokens, notification
connectors (Slack, Discord, webhooks), in-app notifications, and project-specific
audit records.

A solo developer and an enterprise work the same way: an organization with one
member and a project, or one with many members across multiple projects.

## Your organizations and projects

When you first sign up while sign-ups are open, Telmoni provisions:

1. **An Organization**, with you as its **Owner**. Until named, the console
   identifies it by your email address, and its URL uses a generated
   placeholder (see [Slugs](#slugs)).
2. **A Default Project** inside that organization, ready for your API tokens and
   connectors.

You can belong to multiple organizations at once: ones you own and ones you have
been invited to. Your role in one organization does not dictate your role in
another.

### Roles at a glance

Both organizations and projects use three roles: **Owner**, **Admin**, and
**Member**. See [Roles and permissions](/workspace/roles/) for the full
permissions matrix.

- **Organization Owner**: Full access across all projects, project creation and
  deletion, member management, organization settings, and organization
  transfer/deletion.
- **Organization Admin**: Can create projects, manage members and organization
  settings, and read the organization's audit log. Operates as an admin across
  all projects. Cannot delete projects, delete the organization, or transfer
  ownership.
- **Organization Member**: Access is restricted to the specific projects they are
  seated in. No organization-wide management powers.

## Moving between them

The console's address names the organization, and the project under it, by a
**slug**: a short form of the name, such as `acme` for an organization named
Acme. On these pages, `{organization}` and `{project}` stand for those two
slugs.

- **An organization's overview** is `/{organization}`.
- **Organization pages** sit under a literal `~` segment:
  `/{organization}/~/projects`, `/{organization}/~/members`,
  `/{organization}/~/audit-log`, and `/{organization}/~/settings`.
- **Project pages** sit under the organization: `/{organization}/{project}` is
  the project's overview, with `/api-keys`, `/connectors`, `/members`,
  `/audit-log`, and `/settings` under it.
- **Account pages** live under `/account`, including `/account/settings`,
  `/account/notifications`, and `/account/privacy`.
- **Switch between projects and organizations** using the switcher in the
  console header. It lists your organizations and the projects within each.

### Slugs

Telmoni derives a slug from the name whenever the name is set; you do not
choose it separately.

- A slug is lowercase ASCII letters and digits, in words joined by hyphens, at
  most 48 characters long: `Müller GmbH` becomes `muller-gmbh`.
- An organization's slug is unique across all organizations, ones awaiting
  deletion included; once a deleted organization is purged, another
  organization may take its slug. A project's is unique within its
  organization.
- A slug that is already taken gets a number, up to `-20`: `acme-2`. Past
  that, an organization's slug ends in six random characters instead
  (`acme-k3x9qz`), and a project's name is treated as one that gives no slug
  (below).
- Some words are reserved for the console's own paths (`account`, `api`,
  `settings`, and others); an organization named one gets the numbered form.
- An organization nobody has named, and a name that gives no slug (one with no
  Latin letters or digits), get a placeholder: `org-` or `project-` followed by
  ten random characters. On a rename, such a name keeps the slug already held.
- **A rename moves the URL.** The address of every page under the renamed
  organization or project changes, and the old address answers **Not found**.
  Pages open in the console follow the rename; links made before it do not.
- **IDs never move.** An organization's ID (`org_...`) and a project's ID
  (`project_...`) are unchanged by a rename, and a console path spelled with an
  ID in place of a slug redirects to the current slugs, keeping the rest of the
  path. Use the ID in anything you keep. A project's ID is shown on its
  **Settings** page and on the organization's **Projects** page; an
  organization's by the [CLI](/api/cli/#list-organizations) and by
  [`GET /v1/organization`](/api/reference/#get-v1organization).

### Console navigation and keyboard shortcuts

The web console provides built-in keyboard navigation (`⌘` is `Ctrl` on
Windows and Linux):

- **Quick search (`⌘K` or `/`)**: Opens the search palette: recently visited
  pages, projects, documentation, and, on a project's pages, that project's API
  keys and members.
- **Ask the agent (`⌘J`)**: Opens the [AI Context Agent](/self-host/agent/)
  panel from any project view. Ask questions about project activity, members,
  connectors, or audit logs. Responses stream with inline citations and links
  to referenced sources. Past conversations are saved per user and can be
  reopened or deleted.
- **Account menu (`⌘⇧K`)**: Opens the user menu: your account, the
  documentation, the keyboard shortcuts sheet, theme and corners, and sign out.
- **Primary action (`c`)**: Triggers the primary action of the current page:
  **New project**, **Mint key**, or **Invite member**.
- **Navigation sequences (`g` followed by a key)**, relative to the project
  you are in:
  - `g` `o`: Overview
  - `g` `k`: API keys
  - `g` `c`: Connectors
  - `g` `m`: Members
  - `g` `l`: Audit log
  - `g` `p`: Settings
  - `g` `O`: The organization's overview, from anywhere in the console

  On an organization's own pages, `g` `o`, `g` `m`, `g` `l`, and `g` `p` open
  the organization's Overview, Members, Audit log, and Settings, and `g` `k`
  and `g` `c` do nothing.
- **Keyboard shortcuts cheat sheet (`?`)**: Opens the full shortcuts sheet, with
  a switch to enable or disable single-key shortcuts.

## Projects

### Creating a project

An organization Owner or Admin can create a new project from the organization's
**Projects** page (`/{organization}/~/projects`) or from the switcher:

1. Select **New project** on the Projects page, or **Create project** in the
   switcher.
2. Provide a project name: up to 100 characters, and not one another project in
   the organization already has (compared without regard to case).
3. The project is created immediately. Its API keys and connectors are its own.

### Renaming a project

An Admin or Owner can rename a project from the project's **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/{project}/settings`). The same limits apply as at creation,
and the rename moves the project's URL (see [Slugs](#slugs)).

### Deleting a project

Only the **Owner** can delete a project. Deletion is initiated from the
**Danger zone** on the project's **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/{project}/settings`):

- It takes effect at once, with no restore window.
- Deleting a project deletes its API keys, member seats, and pending
  invitations with it, and its connectors are purged right after.
- Historical audit events for that project remain preserved in the organization's
  append-only audit chain.

### Transferring a project

An organization Owner can hand a project to one of the project's Admins:

1. The Owner selects **Transfer project** on the Admin's row on the project's
   **Members** page (`/{organization}/{project}/members`).
2. The Admin receives a transfer offer, which stands for **7 days**
   (`OFFER_TTL_DAYS`). The Owner can withdraw it before it is accepted.
3. The Admin accepts the transfer into an organization they own.
4. On acceptance:
   - The project row moves into the new organization. It keeps its name unless
     that organization already has a project by it, in which case it arrives
     under a numbered name (`Payments 2`). Its URL changes with the move (see
     [Slugs](#slugs)).
   - Its API keys follow into the new organization.
   - Project members stay seated on the project and are enrolled into the new
     organization.
   - The former owner remains seated as a project Admin.
   - **Connectors are purged**: because connectors store webhook secrets and
     third-party grants, they do not follow across tenant boundaries.

## Organizations

### Renaming an organization

An Owner or Admin can rename an organization from its **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/~/settings`). The name can be up to 80 characters, and the
rename moves the URL of the organization and of every project in it (see
[Slugs](#slugs)).

### Transferring organization ownership

An organization has exactly one Owner. The Owner can transfer ownership to any
organization Admin:

1. The Owner selects **Transfer ownership** on the Admin's row on the
   organization's **Members** page (`/{organization}/~/members`). The
   organization must have a name first: until it has one it is shown by its
   owner's address, which would change hands with it.
2. The offer stands for **7 days**. The Owner may withdraw the offer at any time
   before it is accepted.
3. The designated Admin can accept or decline the offer, from the console bell
   or their account's **Notifications** page (`/account/notifications`).
4. Upon acceptance:
   - The accepting Admin becomes the new **Owner**.
   - The former owner becomes an **Admin** in the organization.
   - All projects, API keys, connectors, and audit histories stay intact.

### Deleting an organization

Only the **Owner** can delete an organization. Deletion is performed from the
**Danger zone** in the organization's **Settings** page
(`/{organization}/~/settings`):

1. Request a 6-digit confirmation code, which is emailed to the Owner's address
   and remains valid for 15 minutes.
2. Enter the confirmation code.
3. **Access ends immediately**: the organization enters `pending_deletion`, all
   API keys stop authenticating, and member access ceases.
4. **14-day restore window**: For 14 days (`RESTORE_WINDOW_SECONDS`), the Owner
   can restore the organization from their account's **Privacy** page
   (`/account/privacy`).
5. After 14 days, the automated deletion sweep permanently purges the
   organization, its projects, API keys, and resources.

## Leaving an organization or project

- **Leaving an organization**: Anyone but the Owner can leave from the
  organization's **Overview** page (`/{organization}`), with **Leave
  organization**. Leaving removes you from the organization and from every
  project in it.
- **Leaving a project**: The organization's **Projects** page
  (`/{organization}/~/projects`) offers **Leave** on each project you hold a
  seat on. That page opens only for the organization's Owner and Admins.
- The **Owner cannot leave** an active organization. To leave, the Owner must
  either transfer ownership to an Admin or delete the organization.
